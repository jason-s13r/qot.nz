Egg Hunt

Co-opEaster

Hunt for chocolate eggs in a Kiwi garden on Easter morning, and bring them back to the picnic blanket before lunch.

What it is

Egg Hunt is a co-op Easter game for kids, made in New Zealand. It’s Easter morning in a Kiwi autumn garden. Search 19 spots, from the flower bed to the garden shed, find the chocolate eggs, and bring them back to the picnic blanket before lunch. Not every spot has an egg. Play on your own, or with friends in a private room.

It runs in a web browser on phones, tablets and computers, in portrait or landscape, and you can add it to your home screen to play offline.

Getting ready

Every hunt starts in the dress-up room. Pick your hair, skin, hat, shirt, pants, suit and basket. Some baskets hold more eggs, some let you move faster, and some help with the games. New clothes cost eggs in the shop, and a few spots in the garden hide clothes instead of chocolate.

In the garden

  • Searching. Tap a plant or anything else in the garden to search it. You might find mini eggs, chocolate eggs, chocolate bunnies, or a golden egg.
  • Empty spots. Some spots have nothing in them. Try somewhere else and come back later.
  • Waiting. Once you’ve searched a spot, it takes about a minute and a half before it’s worth searching again. Late in the hunt some spots are picked clean for good.
  • Full basket. Your basket has a limit. When it’s full, take your eggs to the picnic blanket.
  • Banking. Eggs only count once they’re on the blanket. Anything still in your basket at lunchtime is lost.
  • Lunchtime. The hunt ends when the clock runs out. You can also tap Leave to head back early with your basket.

Garden games

Sometimes a spot turns into a mini-game. Win for bonus eggs; lose and you end up muddy. The games include catching falling eggs, finding the bunny, shooing pūkeko off the hot cross buns, sorting eggs, digging for the golden egg, mixing the hot cross buns, patting cats, winding up toys, and fixing the Easter picture.

Playing with friends

Tap Play with friends to make a room, and friends join by typing its five-letter code on their own devices. Everyone searches the same garden and puts their finds into one shared basket, which everybody gets at lunchtime. Some spots need two of you to search them properly. The host can lock the room once everyone’s in.

After the hunt

The results show how many eggs you found, with an award for each player. Spend your eggs in the dress-up shop, and collect stickers in your sticker book for spots searched, games won and hunts finished.

For grown-ups

  • No accounts, names or emails. Each device gets a random key, and progress is saved to the game’s server under that key.
  • Rooms are private and can be locked. There’s no typed chat, only emotes, and each player gets a made-up name from the game’s own list.
  • A Google ad may appear after a hunt. Ads are marked as for a children’s site, so they aren’t personalised.
  • Keep safe, behind a grown-up question, lets you move a profile to another device, make a backup code, turn ads off, or delete everything.

The full details are on the Egg Hunt privacy page.

A Kiwi Easter

Egg Hunt is the Easter sibling of Trick or Treat and Summer Santa. Easter in New Zealand comes in autumn, so the garden has feijoas, flax and tree ferns, pūkeko poking about, hot cross buns on the go, and gumboots by the back door.
