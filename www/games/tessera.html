What it is

Tessera is a strategy game about territory. Every empire starts from its capital somewhere on a big grid, and every tile on the map is up for grabs. Push out to claim empty land, attack where you’re strong, and outflank a neighbour before they outflank you. Spread too thin and you’ll be picked apart; sit still and the map fills up without you. At the end, whoever holds the most ground wins.

It’s a browser recreation of Empire Attack, the online grid game by Ian Andrew and Jon Grove that ran from 2008 to 2017. You can read about the original on Ian Andrew’s Empire Attack page. Tessera is an independent recreation and isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by its creators.

Games that last for days

Tessera games keep running while everybody is away, so a game can last for days. Drop in, make your moves, and come back later to see what your neighbours did overnight.

You can close the tab and stay away as long as you like: your empire keeps its place until the game’s time runs out, so nobody loses their empire for going to bed. The Length setting decides when the game ends, and people can join during the first part of it. Finished games are kept so anyone can watch them back.

Setting up a game

Play now starts a solo game against three bots straight away. New game sets up a solo or online game, and an online game gives you a link to send to friends. You choose:

  • Map size: small, medium or large.
  • Empires for people and players per empire, so friends can each run their own empire or team up on one.
  • Bot empires, computer-run rivals to fill out the map.
  • When it starts: straight away, or at a set time so everyone can be there.

On the map

Pan around the grid with the arrow buttons and zoom in and out to see the whole map or just your front line. Map and Standings show the big picture and who’s ahead, and you can pause or leave a game at any time.

Your name

There are no accounts. Pick a name in Settings and it’s shown as name#fingerprint: anybody can choose any name, so the fingerprint is what tells two players with the same name apart. Your name is kept in your browser and shared with each game you join.

Tessera sets no cookies and shows no ads. Online games, including their chat, are deleted 30 days after they finish. The full details are on the Tessera privacy page.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes.
Can I still play Empire Attack?
No. Empire Attack closed in 2017 and isn’t playable any more, which is part of why Tessera exists. Ian Andrew’s Empire Attack page has more about the original.
Can I see where Tessera started?
Yes. The first Tessera prototype, where the basic gameplay was worked out, is still playable at tessera.qot.nz/prototype.
Is the code available?
Yes, it’s on GitHub at jason-s13r/grid-games.
