What it is

Trick or Treat is a co-op Halloween game for kids, made in New Zealand. You dress up, head out onto a spooky street of 19 houses, and knock on as many doors as you can before midnight. Most houses hand over a treat. A few play a trick instead, and you’ll have to beat it. Play on your own, or with friends in a private room.

It runs in a web browser on phones, tablets and computers, in portrait or landscape, and you can add it to your home screen to play offline.

Getting dressed

Every night starts in the dress-up room. Pick your hair, skin, hat, shirt, pants, robe and basket. Baskets aren’t just for show: some hold more lollies, some let you walk faster, and some help with the tricks. New clothes cost lollies in the shop, and every now and then a house hands out clothes instead of a treat.

On the street

  • Treats. Most houses give you something for your basket: a lolly, a chocolate, a chocolate fish, or if you’re lucky a giant lollipop.
  • Full basket. When your basket is full, nobody can give you any more, so head home and empty it.
  • Banking. Lollies only count once they’re banked at home. Anything still in your basket at midnight is lost.
  • Porch lights. After you knock, that house’s light goes off for about a minute and a half, so keep moving. Late in the night some houses go to bed for good, so plan your route.
  • Midnight. The night ends when the clock runs out. You can also tap Leave to head home early.

The tricks

Some doors open on a mini-game. Win it for bonus lollies, lose it and you get slimed. There are nineteen of them:

catching falling pumpkins, closing the vampire’s curtains, stirring the witch’s cauldron, finding the hiding ghost, unwrapping the mummy, rebuilding the skeleton, patting cats, popping spider webs, matching potions, sneaking past the werewolf, winding up toys, counting bats, bonking zombies, sorting lollies, lighting lanterns, shooing crows, digging for treasure, echoing moreporks, and fixing the portrait in the haunted mansion.

Playing with friends

Tap Play with friends to make a room, and your friends join by typing its five-letter code on their own devices. Everyone walks the same street and shares the porch lights, and everything you bank goes into one shared lolly jar that everybody gets at midnight. Some “friend doors” only open when two of you are on the porch together. The host can lock the room once everyone’s in.

After the night

The results show how many lollies you banked, with an award for each player. Spend your lollies in the dress-up shop, and fill your sticker book with stickers for houses visited, tricks beaten and nights played.

For grown-ups

  • No accounts, names or emails. Each device gets a random key, and progress is saved to the game’s server under that key.
  • Rooms are private and can be locked. There’s no typed chat, only friendly emotes, and players get made-up spooky names from the game’s own list.
  • A Google ad may appear between nights. Ads are marked as for a children’s site, so they aren’t personalised.
  • Keep safe, behind a grown-up question, lets you move a profile to another device, make a backup code, turn ads off, or delete everything.

The full details are on the Trick or Treat privacy page.

A Kiwi Halloween

The game was designed on paper by a young game designer and built in New Zealand, so it’s a local Halloween: lollies rather than candy, a haunted dairy, a morepork in the trees, and gumboots, chilly bins and kete among the baskets.
